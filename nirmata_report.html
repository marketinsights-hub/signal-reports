Nirmata — February 2026
What You Say You Do

“The AI Platform Engineering Assistant.” You built Kyverno—the open-source Kubernetes policy engine now hosted by the CNCF—and earned genuine technical respect in cloud-native infrastructure. Now you’re reaching for something bigger: a pivot from policy tool to AI-powered platform engineering. You even named the move yourself—“Moving Beyond Kyverno to AI Platform Engineering.” The ambition is real. The question is whether the market heard the new name.

The Name That Doesn’t Travel

Nirmata calls itself “The AI Platform Engineering Assistant” on its homepage. That name appears nowhere else—not on its own pricing page, not in analyst coverage, not in press articles, not on Reddit, not on Hacker News, and not even in how its CEO introduces himself to the Kubernetes community. The product has evolved. The market’s label hasn’t.

The Pattern

The Name That Doesn’t Travel

Nirmata has built something that matters—Kyverno is one of the most adopted policy engines in the Kubernetes ecosystem. The technical achievement is real, and the pivot ambition toward AI-powered platform engineering shows genuine strategic vision.

But the new name hasn’t left the homepage. On the same page, the FAQ calls it “the enterprise control plane that enhances Kyverno.” The pricing page describes “Policy-as-Code, powered by Kyverno”—no mention of AI. The CEO introduces himself on Reddit as “a Kyverno maintainer and also lead Nirmata.” An analyst calls it “a Kubernetes policy engine.” The press calls it “the company that commercializes Kyverno.” An enterprise team blogged about adopting Kyverno for production—scaling challenges and all—without mentioning Nirmata once. On AWS Marketplace, the enterprise listing has zero reviews.

The AI identity is also structurally gated: the AI agents that define the “AI Platform Engineering Assistant” positioning are only available in the most expensive tier. The identity the company markets is a feature most customers won’t access.

Triangulated across Hacker News, The New Stack, Intellyx, Adevinta Tech Blog, AWS Marketplace, Reddit, and a voice audit of 10 company web pages—7 independent sources, 26 data points

What This Signals

The CNCF open-source-to-enterprise path has claimed two companies in 18 months—Styra (OPA) and Weaveworks (Flux). Both had community trust, technical respect, and hosted projects. Neither built a commercial identity that could stand independently from the project.

Meanwhile, Kubernetes itself is shipping native admission policy features that overlap with Kyverno’s core. The window for Nirmata to build brand equity that doesn’t depend on the OSS project is compressing. The companies that survive this shakeout won’t be the ones with the best open-source projects—they’ll be the ones whose name travels beyond the GitHub README.
