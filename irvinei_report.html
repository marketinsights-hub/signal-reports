After a major competitor was caught lying about local storage, after the market leader won an anti-award for AI surveillance at CES 2026, and after 87% of doorbell buyers reported not knowing how their data is used, trust is the scarcest resource in this category.

What This Signals

Signals across smart home communities show a growing migration toward verifiably local, vendor-independent solutions. Users are building DIY alternatives with Frigate and Home Assistant — tolerating steep learning curves to escape subscription lock-in and broken privacy promises.

The gap between "I want true local AI" and "I can build it myself" is a product opportunity that no credible player has claimed. But credible is the operative word. The company that fills this gap must pass the trust test that two competitors have already failed.

A product positioning as "the world's first AI Home Hub" — with 23 patents and a CES 2026 presence — has generated zero organic community discussion. The market hasn't rejected OVAL. It hasn't noticed OVAL. When it does, the first test won't be features or price — it will be trust.

The Opening

Resolving the privacy/cloud contradiction before scaling — by restructuring how AI features are delivered (local-first, cloud-optional) — would make OVAL the first smart doorbell brand to credibly own the "verifiably private" position that Eufy forfeited and Ring never claimed.

The trust gap in this category is a positioning asset, not a liability — if the business model matches the promise.
