The Pattern

The Seventeen-Named Company

When a product 100 million people call “my notetaker” starts calling itself 17 different things, the identity has fractured.

Your website uses different voices on nearly every page. The Education page uses pet metaphors. The Recruiting page uses divine language. The Media page still contains recruiting-specific copy. And the footer of every page—including the autonomous SDR Agent—reads “The world’s smartest AI notetaker.”

The market responded with one metaphor: infection. “Acts exactly like a virus.” “Invaded my entire company.” “We called it the Otter Infection.” Products that try to be everything lose their boundaries—and products without boundaries spread without permission.

Where Otter Sits

Based on user sentiment—not marketing claims:

“Feature-rich but overwhelming” — where Otter sits today

“Simple and focused” — where Fathom and Fireflies gain ground

“Built-in, good enough” — Zoom and Teams native AI

The user who said “I just want live transcriptions—I don’t need too many features” represents the market’s center of gravity. Otter is accelerating away from it.

What This Signals

The AI meeting space is splitting into tools and agents. Companies that grew to $100M as tools face an identity tax when they become agents—their users chose them because they were tools.

The companies that name what they’re becoming before their users name it for them will own the next category. Otter’s users have already chosen a name. It isn’t any of the 17.
